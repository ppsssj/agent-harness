# Design Research

## Use when

Creating a meaningful greenfield visual surface, substantially changing a visual direction, or when a product lacks a settled design system.

## Do not use when

A small UI change is governed by an existing system or a visual direction is already documented and sufficient.

## Inputs

Product and audience, surface and primary task, existing system, brand/accessibility/performance constraints, and visual ambition.

## Workflow

1. Inspect product context and existing conventions. Decide whether reference research is needed.
2. Route the task with [design discovery sources](../../references/design-sources.md); favor product references for product UX.
3. Select a few useful references and decompose their hierarchy, type, grid, composition, spacing, color, navigation, motion, interaction, content patterns, strengths, and adaptation risks.
4. Create a [Reference Lock](../../templates/reference-lock.md) when the work is significant.
5. Synthesize an original direction constrained by requirements and the existing system; capture it in [DESIGN.md](../../templates/DESIGN.md) where durable.

## Stop conditions

Stop when the direction is actionable and traceable, local conventions make external research unnecessary, or a product/brand decision needs human input. Never copy a reference's distinctive layout or identity.

## Output and verification

Return selected sources, decomposed observations, lock, design dials, original direction, and open risks. Verify each cited reference supports its stated lesson and that no borrowed concept becomes imitation.
